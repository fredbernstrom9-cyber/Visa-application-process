                  Method

                  How we built the rulebook

                  1. Scope. 43 passports × 27 EU destinations. Eight destinations (France, Germany, the Netherlands, Spain, Italy, Ireland, Poland, Sweden) have full guides; the other 19 have basic guides linked to each country's official study-permit page. Schengen short-stay rules apply to all.
                  2. Source hierarchy. EU law and the responsible authority's own page first; then the destination's embassy or consulate in the applicant's country; then universities and national study agencies. Secondary sources (news, law firms) appear only where an official source agrees.
                  3. Both sides of the trip. For each passport we searched each destination's foreign-service website (diplo.de, esteri.it, exteriores.gob.es, gov.pl, ireland.ie, swedenabroad.se, netherlandsandyou.nl, France-Visas) and list 150 embassy and consulate pages. Where we found no country-specific page, the checker shows the destination's worldwide portal instead. We also list the foreign ministry of every passport country and, for US, UK, Canadian and Australian passports, their government's travel advice for each destination.
                  4. Numbered citations. Every fact in a result carries a number in square brackets. The numbers are listed under Sources at the bottom of that result, together with the embassy and government links for the chosen countries.
                  5. Confidence labels. Key figures carry one: Official source 2+ sources Verify. "Verify" means we found one source only, or sources disagree.
                  6. Date. Everything was checked on 24 Sep 2026. Dated changes go into the change log.
                  7. Limitation. Our research tool could not open government websites directly (its network blocked them). We therefore checked each fact through web searches restricted to official government and EU domains, reading the official pages' text as the search engine returned it. Where that did not confirm an exact figure, the fact is labelled "Verify". Open the linked official page before you rely on any figure.

                  Corrections

                  What verification changed

                  Re-checking every fact against official pages corrected our first version in these places:

                  • France: validating the student VLS-TS now costs €150, not €50 (taxes raised on 1 May 2026).
                  • Poland: the national visa fee is €200 since 1 January 2026, not €80.
                  • Italy: the post-study job-search permit lasts 9–12 months, not a flat 12.
                  • Sweden: the maintenance requirement is counted over 10 months a year, not 12.
                  • Germany: Brazilian citizens can also enter visa-free and apply for a student permit in Germany.
                  • Ireland: new courses no longer join the ILEP list; providers now need TrustEd Ireland authorisation.
                  • Spain: the student visa fee varies by nationality, and the length of the post-study permit could not be confirmed, so it is marked "Verify".
                  • Refusal rates: we removed figures that came only from secondary sources and kept the European Commission's 2025 statistics.
                  • Tuition estimates were removed. Fees differ by institution and programme, so the checker shows only official government fees.
